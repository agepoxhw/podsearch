Hubs and the math shortcut, in plain words

Every number and chart below comes from your real index (540 chunks, 3 episodes). No algebra is needed to follow it. Where there is a formula, a small example with real numbers sits next to it.

The short version.
  1. Some 10-second chunks are music or noise. The model turns them into a "nothing in particular" vector that looks a little bit similar to everything, so they show up for every search.
  2. We give each chunk a hub score: how similar it is, on average, to 40 unrelated topics ("banana bread", "chess", ...). Real speech scores low. Hubs score high.
  3. The API hides chunks with a hub score above 0.17.
  4. The shortcut: instead of comparing each chunk to 40 topics, compare it to one pre-made "average topic" arrow. Same answer, 40 times cheaper, and it can run while the audio is embedded.

1. What is a vector, and what is "similarity"?

The model turns each 10-second piece of audio into a list of 1024 numbers. Think of it as an arrow pointing somewhere in space. A text query ("Iran") also becomes an arrow. Cosine similarity is just "how much do the two arrows point the same way": 1 means identical direction, 0 means unrelated, negative means opposite.

That is the number you see as the search score. Our threshold is 0.20: hits below it are not returned.

2. The problem: hubs

Music, jingles and noise do not have a meaning for the model to point at, so it gives them a bland arrow that sits roughly in the middle of all topics. A bland arrow in the middle is slightly similar to every query, which is how a chunk of pure music ended up as the top answer for almost anything you typed (0.479 for ep310 at 7:00 earlier).

3. The test: ask 40 unrelated questions

A real speech chunk is about something: it is similar to "Iran", not to "banana bread", "chess" and "jazz" at once. A hub is a little similar to all of them. So:

hub score = the average of the chunk's similarity to 40 unrelated topics.

This is what your 540 chunks look like. The tall grey hump is normal speech. The red bars on the right are the hubs:

Numbers: average score 0.030, spread (standard deviation) 0.048. The cut-off 0.17 is "average + 3 spreads" = 0.030 + 3 x 0.048 = 0.173. The 3 is a rule of thumb: in a hump like this, almost nothing normal lies that far out, so anything beyond it is odd. It hides 8 of 540 chunks.

The top two (ep310 at 7:00 and ep289 at 10:00) are the ones you found by ear, and they stand far apart from everyone else.

4. The shortcut: 40 comparisons become one

The straightforward way: for every chunk, compare with each of the 40 topic arrows and average. That is 40 comparisons per chunk, and it needs the text model to make the 40 arrows.

The trick: averaging comparisons gives the same result as comparing once with the averaged arrow. Compare with an arrow made by averaging the 40 topic arrows. Here it is with just two topics, so you can check it by hand:

This is just "multiplication spreads over addition", like 3 x (2 + 4) = 3x2 + 3x4. Because it is always true, we can build the averaged arrow once, store it (1024 numbers, 10 KB, in probe_centroid.json), and use it forever.

On your real data I checked it: the new score and the old way (40 comparisons) agree with a correlation of 0.9999998, i.e. the same up to rounding.

5. Why the cut-off changed from "z = 3" to "0.17"

Before, we turned scores into z-scores: "how many spreads above the average of everything in the library". That is the same idea, but the average and spread were recomputed from the whole library each time. Add a few music-heavy episodes and the spread grows, every z-score shrinks, and hubs slip under the line without any code change. The cut-off 3 stopped meaning anything fixed.

Now we did the calculation once on today's library, got 0.1734, rounded to 0.17, and froze it. A chunk's score no longer depends on the other chunks. It is a plain setting in the API (HUB_MAX); changing it needs no rewrite of the data. The same 8 chunks are hidden as before.

6. What it does and does not do

  • Does: hides chunks that match everything. Your searches no longer return the music clip.
  • Does not: fix wrong hits that are really about geopolitics. The 5 recurring wrong hits in the Middle East results scored 1.8 to 2.8 on the old scale, below the cut-off. They are talking about related things, so they are not hubs. Lowering the cut-off would also hide good hits.
  • Hides, never deletes. Chunks stay in the index. below_threshold=true in the API shows them with their score.
  • Better root-cause fix (not built): detect music at ingest (voice activity detection) and tag it. It would catch it before the model sees it. The hub score stays useful as a safety net.

7. Where the code is

  • src/podsearch/hubness.py has the two key functions: centroid() (the averaged arrow) and hub_score() (one comparison).
  • src/podsearch/probe_centroid.json is the averaged arrow (make probes regenerates it only if the 40 topics or the model change).
  • ingest/pipeline.py computes the score in the embedding step and stores it as hub in Qdrant.
  • vectors.py search adds one condition: skip chunks with hub above the cut-off.
